Day 1: Introduction to Air-Conditioning

1. What is Air Conditioning?

Air conditioning (AC) is the process of removing heat and controlling the humidity of air in an enclosed space to achieve a comfortable interior environment.

2. Why is AC Required?

  • Thermal Comfort: Increases productivity, improves health, and reduces absenteeism.
  • Process Control: Some manufacturing processes require strict environmental controls where human comfort is secondary.
  • Strict Parameters: Clean rooms, hospital operating theatres, and server rooms require air purity, moisture content, and temperature to be strictly maintained regardless of external weather.

3. Heat Gains

  • Sensible Heat: Heat that changes the temperature without changing moisture. Caused by occupants, lights, machines, conduction, and solar radiation.
  • Latent Heat: Moisture gains from infiltration, occupants (sweating/breathing), and processes. Latent heat changes moisture content, not the thermometer temperature.

An AC system must be capable of: Filtering, heating, cooling, humidifying, dehumidifying, and distributing air evenly.

Day 2: Terminology

1. Key AC Terms

  • HVAC: Heating, Ventilation, and Air Conditioning. The overall system that creates a comfortable indoor environment.
  • BTU (British Thermal Unit): A unit of measurement quantifying the amount of heat energy required to raise/lower the temperature of a given amount of air. Used to size AC capacity.
  • Refrigeration Cycle: The process of circulating a refrigerant, which changes from a gas to a liquid and back to a gas, absorbing indoor heat and releasing it outside.
  • Sensible Heat: Heat energy that causes a change in temperature without a state change (moisture change).
  • Heat Exchanger: A device that transfers heat between two fluids without them coming into direct contact.

Day 3: Equipment Components

1. Core Refrigeration Components

  • Compressor: The heart of the system. Pressurizes the refrigerant gas, raising its temperature and pressure.
  • Condenser: A heat exchanger in the outdoor unit. It receives high-pressure, hot gas from the compressor and releases heat to the outside air, causing the refrigerant to condense into a liquid.
  • Expansion Valve: Regulates the flow of refrigerant. It reduces the pressure, allowing the liquid to expand and cool rapidly before entering the evaporator.
  • Evaporator: A heat exchanger in the indoor unit. Low-pressure liquid evaporates here, absorbing heat from the indoor air.

2. Air Distribution Components

  • Circulation Fan (Blower): Moves air through the indoor unit across the evaporator coil and into the room.
  • Ductwork: Network of channels that distribute conditioned air.
  • Thermostat: Control device to regulate and maintain the desired temperature.
  • Filters: Trap dust and allergens (e.g., HEPA filters) to improve air purity.

Day 4: Psychrometrics and Air Properties

1. What is Psychrometrics?

The study of the physical and thermodynamic properties of air and their relationship to moisture content.

2. Key Parameters

  • Dry-Bulb Temperature: The temperature of the air measured by a regular thermometer. Essential for determining sensible heat transfer.
  • Wet-Bulb Temperature: The lowest temperature achieved by evaporating water into the air. Measured with a thermometer covered in a wetted wick. It indicates the air's moisture content and cooling potential.
  • Relative Humidity (RH): The ratio of actual moisture in the air to the maximum amount it can hold at a given temperature, expressed as a percentage. High RH feels damp; low RH feels dry.
  • Dew Point: The temperature at which air becomes 100% saturated with moisture, causing condensation to form on surfaces.

Day 5: Selection Criteria

1. How to Select an AC System

  • Cooling Load Calculation: Takes into account room size, solar heat gain (windows), occupancy, and equipment to size the AC correctly.
  • Energy Efficiency: Look for a high SEER (Seasonal Energy Efficiency Ratio) rating. E.g., SEER 18 consumes less energy than SEER 14.
  • Noise Levels: Measured in decibels. Bedrooms/offices require quieter systems (e.g., 50 dB vs 70 dB).
  • Environmental Impact: Choose refrigerants with lower Global Warming Potential (GWP) and ozone depletion potential, like R-410A instead of the older R-22.
  • Zoning Options: Allows independent temperature control in different areas/floors.
  • Air Quality Enhancement: Use of HEPA filters to capture fine particles.
  • System Integration: Linking the AC with a Building Management System (BMS) for occupancy-based scheduling.

Day 6: Classification of AC Systems

1. By Purpose

  • Comfort AC: Aims to maximize human comfort (e.g., residential split AC).
  • Industrial AC: Maintains specific temp/humidity for processes (e.g., cleanrooms, server rooms).

2. By Season

  • Summer AC: Cools and dehumidifies outside air.
  • Winter AC: Heats and humidifies (adds moisture/steam) outside air.
  • Composite (All-Weather): Provides both heating and cooling (e.g., Heat Pumps which reverse the refrigeration cycle).

3. By Equipment Arrangement

  • Central AC: A centralized unit (often on the roof) that cools air and distributes it via ducts to multiple rooms.
  • Unitary AC: Self-contained, standalone units (e.g., window AC units) providing localized cooling.

4. By Working Substance

  • All-Air System: Uses air to transport cooling/heating energy through ducts.
  • All-Water System (Chilled Water): Circulates chilled water through pipes to coils. Air blows over the coils to cool the room.
  • Air-Water System: Uses a water loop (like a well or cooling tower) and air handlers (e.g., Water source heat pump).
